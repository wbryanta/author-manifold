## `data/ai-longform/` — AI-generated long-form fiction corpus — CC0 1.0

**1,072 manifest records** of machine-generated long-form output (~3,500-word
target) from 8 models (Claude Fable 5 / Opus 4.8 / Sonnet 4.6 / Haiku 4.5,
GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, Gemma 4 26B, Qwen 3.6 35B) under matched scenarios and
five conditions (unprompted / style-prompted / paraphrase / exemplar /
completion), with a full generation manifest (`manifest.jsonl`). All samples
were generated by the repository owner, 2026-06-09 to 2026-06-11. Model
pinning status per model — including where it is weaker than it should be —
is in [`MODELS.md`](MODELS.md).

The prompts' source materials do not ship: the exemplar condition's
in-context excerpts and the completion condition's source openings (drawn
from in-copyright contemporary novels) are excluded from this release.

### What the 1,072 records actually contain

Not all of them are fiction. Composition (`data/ai-longform/sample_flags.json`,
regenerate with `python3 tools/flag_corpus_samples.py`):

| | Count |
|---|---|
| Records in the manifest | **1,072** |
| — refusal messages, no usable prose (all in the completion condition) | **32** |
| — empty outputs (zero tokens) | **3** |
| — other outputs below the 1,500-MFW-token hard floor | **17** |
| — usable as fiction (everything else) | **1,037** |

By length against the paper's §3.7 floors: 778 compliant (≥3,000 MFW tokens),
242 sub-floor (1,500–2,999), 49 below the hard floor, 3 empty.

A further **21 samples decline the styling instruction and then write
original prose anyway** ("I can't write in X's exact voice, but here's an
original piece…" followed by thousands of words of fiction). All 21 are
OpenAI models. They are real fiction and are counted as styled samples in
the analyses, but they are not attempts at imitation. They are flagged
`declined_styling_then_original` in the sidecar, and the measured
consequence — including that excluding them leaves the published styled rate
and increment inside their intervals — is documented in
`reports/validation/author_space/results2/declined_styling_disclosure.md`.

The refusals are not an accident of the release: refusal behaviour is one of
the paper's reported findings (§5.4 — GPT-5 refused all completions), and the
per-model refusal counts are published in
`reports/validation/author_space/results2/completion_results.md`. The sidecar
exists so that a reader loading the corpus directly can tell which files
those are. `manifest.jsonl` is deliberately **not** modified: it is the
frozen generation record every published number was computed against.

The corpus is published for measurement research, not as creative work.

### Incidental verbatim echo of in-copyright text — audited, with numbers

Because the exemplar and completion conditions put real in-copyright prose in
the model's context (and models memorize), some samples contain short
verbatim echoes of in-copyright text. This section states what was measured,
with what method and thresholds, rather than characterising it.

**Method.** Every one of the 1,072 samples was scanned against the 141
in-copyright novels held locally, word-level, case- and punctuation-
normalised, reporting the longest contiguous matching run per sample.

**Counts are threshold-dependent, so all of them are given:**

| Longest verbatim run | Samples |
|---|---|
| ≥ 19 words | **4** |
| ≥ 12 words | **13** |
| 10–14 words | 46 |
| ≤ 9 words | 1,020 |
| **Longest run anywhere in the corpus** | **30 words** |

The "4" reported in earlier versions of this file and in the paper is the
count at the ≥19-word threshold. Thirteen is the count at ≥12 words. Neither
is more correct; both are given so the number is not mistaken for a
threshold-free fact.

**A previous statement here was wrong and is withdrawn.** This file
previously said "no sample reproduces a source prompt." That is false.
Because the manifest records exactly which slice of each novel was fed to
each model, it can be checked directly, and it was: **12 samples reproduce
between 10 and 30 contiguous words of their own prompt.** The recovered
material concentrates in the prompt opening the model was given (3–18% of the
600-word opening per target) rather than being spread through the novel —
the signature of a model continuing from its context window.

**Substitution-tolerant re-scan (2026-08-06).** Exact-run matching is defeated
by light paraphrase, so a shingle-and-alignment scan was run over all 1,072
samples × 141 novels plus every prompt slice, reporting any span of ≥40
tokens at ≥80% identity or ≥60 tokens at ≥70%. **Zero findings.** The method
was validated on a planted positive (a real 100-token passage with every 8th
word substituted: caught at 88% identity and correctly sourced, where exact
matching found nothing). Re-aligning the 13 known echoes shows they extend by
**at most 7 additional matching words** — they are isolated fragments, not
the visible tips of longer near-copies. Method and full result:
`reports/validation/author_space/results2/near_copy_scan.md`.

**Collection-level bound.** Pooling all 40 completion samples per target, the
union of everything recovered covers **0.27%–0.83% of any source novel**, in
fragments. Reconstruction is not achievable from this corpus.

**Robustness.** Removing all 13 echo-bearing samples leaves every published
rate within its published interval and the sign test unchanged (5/5,
p = 0.031):
`reports/validation/author_space/results2/echo_robustness_13.md`.

**Disposition: keep and defend, not scrub.** The samples ship as generated
because the corpus is a measurement record and silently editing it would
break the correspondence between the published numbers and the released
files. The exception ledger below marks the affected files instead, per
Creative Commons guidance that third-party material should be prominently
marked rather than covered by a corpus-level caveat.

launder any third party's rights in incidentally echoed material.** For the
13 files listed below, the dedication does not reach the echoed fragments of
the source novels; whatever rights subsist in those fragments are unaffected
by anything in this repository.

CC0 is a dedication of **our** rights in the outputs. Each provider's terms
were checked on **2026-08-06**; where a provider's terms condition downstream
use, that is stated rather than papered over.

- **Anthropic** (Claude Fable 5, Opus 4.8, Sonnet 4.6, Haiku 4.5) —
  [Commercial Terms of Service](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms),
  effective 2025-06-17: the customer "owns its Outputs", and Anthropic
  "hereby assigns to Customer its right, title and interest (if any) in and
  to Outputs." Rights in these outputs are the repository owner's to
  dedicate.
- **OpenAI** (GPT-5, GPT-5-mini) —
  [Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/):
  "you (a) retain your ownership rights in Input and (b) own the Output. We
  hereby assign to you all our right, title, and interest, if any, in and to
  Output." Same basis.
- **Google Gemma 4 26B** (local run via Ollama) —
  [Gemma Terms of Use](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/terms), last modified
  2026-04-01: "Google claims no rights in Outputs you generate using Gemma",
  and "Outputs are not deemed Model Derivatives." The Gemma use restrictions
  (§3.2 and the Prohibited Use Policy) must be propagated when distributing

  do not travel with this corpus. Note that Gemma is not an OSI-approved open
  source licence: Google may update the terms, so a reader relying on this
  should check the current version at the link above.
- **Qwen 3.6 35B** (local run via Ollama) — the model

## `data/pd_shelf/` — public-domain calibration shelf — Public Domain

35 novels by 9 authors, all first published before 1930 and **public domain
in the United States**. Plain-text editions from Project Gutenberg
(https://www.gutenberg.org), trimmed to body-only prose by
`tools/build_pd_shelf.py` (deterministic; offsets recorded in
`data/pd_manifest.yaml`). Per-work Gutenberg eBook numbers are recorded in
each file's `# Source:` header and in `data/pd_shelf/pd_shelf_inventory.json`.

The Project Gutenberg trademark license applies to the *trademark*, not the
texts; the texts themselves are public domain. Gutenberg boilerplate has
been removed, so these files are plain public-domain text with provenance
headers. If you redistribute, retaining the provenance headers is requested
as a courtesy.

**The license is shipped: [`data/pd_shelf/GUTENBERG_LICENSE.txt`](data/pd_shelf/GUTENBERG_LICENSE.txt)**
(retrieved 2026-08-06, unmodified). Every shelf file's `# Source:` header
names Project Gutenberg, which is a use of the trademark, and the trademark
license is conditioned on shipping the license text — so it ships, in full.
Project Gutenberg's own policy is that a text stripped of the license and its
references is "a text unrestricted by U.S. intellectual property law", which
is what our trimmed body-only files are; the license is included so the
condition is satisfied either way, without editing the 35 shelf files (their
content hashes are recorded in `data/pd_manifest.yaml` and referenced by the
space artifacts, so editing them would break the provenance chain).

If you are outside the United States, check the copyright status of
pre-1930 works in your jurisdiction.

## `data/pd_work_baselines/` and `data/artifacts/` — derived aggregates

Per-work baseline JSONs and author-space artifacts contain only **aggregate
statistics**: 18 scalar stylometric dimensions per work and z-scored
relative frequencies of the 300 most frequent words. No passages of any
source text are reproduced.

- `author_space_pd_v1.json` is derived entirely from the public-domain
  shelf above.
- `author_space_v1_wave2.json` / `author_space_v1_wave2_fwonly.json` are
  derived from 78 in-copyright contemporary novels (15 authors) that are
  **not** included in this repository. What ships is the measurement
  artifact only: per-work z-scored frequency vectors of common words plus
  scalar dimension values — aggregate statistics, not reproductions of the
  works. Work titles and relative build paths are retained as provenance;
  the referenced text files are not part of this release. To rebuild these
  artifacts you must supply your own copies of the works.
